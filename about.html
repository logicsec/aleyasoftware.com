How we operate

Four rules we do not bend.

Most of what goes wrong on a low-voltage job is not technical. It is a change nobody agreed to, a drawing that never existed, or a system only one person understands. These four exist to stop that.

01

Nothing moves until you have signed off on it.

Anything that can take a live system down — a reboot, a firmware push, a firewall change, re-terminating a run that something is plugged into — gets agreed in writing before it happens. We do not work from assumptions about what you can afford to have offline.

  • Scope defined and agreed before any change
  • Written sign-off for anything that interrupts service
  • Cutover windows picked by you, not by us

02

The drawing is what we build from.

Not a mood board and not a verbal. Floor plan, device schedule, port map, VLAN layout and an itemized bill of materials, all in one place that travels with the job. When a question comes up in a ceiling at 4pm, the answer is on the drawing.

  • Floor plan, topology and VLAN map in one place
  • Itemized bill of materials before anything is ordered
  • Cable schedule and port maps agreed up front

03

We do not build systems we could not maintain.

One crew runs the cabling, the network, the cameras and the doors — no subcontractor layer you never met. If the only reason a building stays online is the person who wired it, that is a failure, not job security. Documentation gets written alongside the work, not after it.

  • Every copper run certified · labeled both ends
  • One crew · no mystery subcontractors
  • Documentation written as the work happens

04

When we leave, it is yours.

Credentials, as-built drawings, port maps and test results are handed over — not held as leverage. You should be able to put this site in front of any other contractor tomorrow and have them pick it up without starting over. That is the whole test.

  • As-built drawings, runbook and test reports
  • A record of every change we made
  • Admin credentials are yours to keep
